## Problem Statement

Independent coworking spaces in India (1–3 locations) take bookings over WhatsApp, phone and spreadsheets, and pay aggregators a large commission for customers they could serve directly. Many also lack a credible website. Cowos gives each space its own branded website and booking app. Customers pay the space directly through the space's own Razorpay account, with whatever fees or charges the space chooses. Cowos takes 0% commission on bookings. SSPL charges each tenant only its share of the real AWS hosting cost, plus messaging at cost: no subscription, no plan fee. The full product definition is the requirements pack in `docs/requirements/` (start with `00-start-here.md`).

## Demand Evidence

- 1–3 casual conversations with coworking owners, plus SSPL's own thesis drawn from Directoo's restaurant model.
- No paid commitments, pilot sign-ups, or workflow data from any named space yet.
- Status: **unverified**. The product owner has decided to build regardless (see Premise 3).

## Status Quo

Not observed directly. The owner's view ("Actually I dont know but all the 3 options could be the case"):
- Bookings over WhatsApp, calls and Excel, with no online payment.
- Aggregator listings that take commission and own the customer.
- A basic website, with enquiries still handled by hand.
- Existing coworking software is less common.

Landscape (web search, generic terms):
- A 2023 founder interview reports myHQ takes 25–30% per booking.
- India-first operations tools already exist (Meshfloor, Engage Apps, CoWorkingOps, Omnify), so "cheap + GST" is not a unique position.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Beachhead:** independent 1–3 location coworking spaces in one city, with a meaningful share of bookings from aggregators. Hyderabad was suggested. The city is not yet confirmed.
- **Wedge:** the full website + booking app (owner's choice). No smaller paid wedge is planned.
- **Pilot:** the first 20 owners in the beachhead city get one year fully free (rules in Pilot Rules below).
- **Expansion gate:** open a second city when 10 pilots each take 10 or more paid online bookings a month for 3 consecutive months.

## Constraints

- Fixed stack: ASP.NET Core (.NET LTS) API, React + TypeScript, PostgreSQL, AWS ap-south-1.
- "0% commission" means Cowos takes no share of a tenant's booking revenue. Customer pricing, promo codes and fees are the tenant's decision.
- Tenants create their own promo codes in the Tenant Admin.
- SSPL revenue model: tenants pay only their share of the real AWS hosting cost plus messaging at cost. No subscription, plan fee or commission (see Premise 5 and Pilot Rules #10).
- Pilot terms: 12 months fully free for the first 20 owners, messaging included within a fair-use cap, no other commitments required (see Pilot Rules).
- Team and timeline as in the pack: about 7–8 people, MVP in about 5–6 months.

## Reviewer Concerns

### R3-1 — feasibility (blocking)

**Problem**

> Premise 5 replaces fixed plans with a variable monthly charge: that month's AWS bill, taken from the Cost and Usage Report, divided by active tenants. The design does not say how this charge is collected or when. The pack's platform billing is built on Razorpay Subscriptions with UPI Autopay, e-mandate or card (00 §C4, 12 §2). Subscriptions charge a fixed plan amount each cycle, so a variable amount needs per-cycle add-ons or a different recurring-charge mechanism, with a mandate maximum and RBI pre-debit notice. The month's AWS bill is final only after the month ends, so the charge is in arrears. Yet the design gives no invoice date, due date, or estimate-and-true-up rule, and it says 'no subscription' while still relying on dunning (Pilot Rules #7). There is also a clash for non-pilot tenants who start paying after the 14-day trial: BRD §7.3 enables Activation, and with it custom domains, only when the first invoice is paid. With billing in arrears, that first invoice arrives about a month after the trial ends.

**Remedy**

> Choose the collection mechanism: a Razorpay Subscription with a nominal plan plus per-cycle add-ons, token or e-mandate recurring charges, or invoice plus payment link. State the billing timing (arrears, or an estimate with a true-up), the invoice and due dates, and the mandate maximum. Redefine what moves a non-pilot from Trial to Active (for example, setting up a mandate or payment method) so that custom domains and indexing are not delayed until the first arrears invoice is paid.

### R3-2 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> Pilot Rules #10 defines the charge as the platform's AWS bill divided by active tenants, but leaves both inputs undefined. The cost pool: does it include only production, or also the staging account (+30–40% per 08 §14), dev, CI, the marketing site and Super Admin? Are credits, Savings Plans and taxes in it? The denominator: 'active' is not mapped to the FR-PL-062 states. It is unclear whether Trial, PastDue, Restricted, Suspended or retention-period tenants count, how SSPL's own test tenant and demo tenants count, and whether a tenant live for part of a month is prorated. Each choice changes every tenant's invoice. For example, excluding trial and unpaid tenants makes paying tenants fund their AWS costs, which conflicts with charging only 'their share'.

**Remedy**

> Define the cost pool (which AWS accounts and cost lines are included) and the denominator (which tenant states count, how partial months are handled, and how SSPL's test and demo tenants are treated). Also state who absorbs the share of tenants that don't pay.

### R3-3 — consistency (blocking)

**Problem**

> S3 storage, CloudFront transfer and SES email are already in the AWS bill that is split equally. Charging a tenant again for 'storage over quota' bills that cost twice, unless the over-quota part is first removed from the shared pool. That contradicts 'only their share of the real AWS hosting cost'. The quotas and 'fair use' thresholds for non-pilot tenants are also undefined. FR-PL-030 has a per-tenant storage quota but no value, and the only fair-use cap in the design is the pilot messaging cap.

**Remedy**

> State the storage quota and any other fair-use thresholds that apply to all tenants. Specify that usage billed per tenant is removed from the pool before the equal split, or drop the separate usage line, so that no AWS cost is charged twice.

### R3-4 — completeness (blocking)

**Problem**

> The messaging billing model is unclear. The pack uses a prepaid wallet debited per message (BRD §7.1, FR-PL-040, FR-TA-175), and pilots use an SSPL-funded wallet (Pilot Rules #4). Pilot Rules #10, however, puts 'messaging at cost' on the monthly charge, after usage 'beyond fair use'. The design does not say whether non-pilot messaging is prepaid or added to the monthly invoice afterwards. It doesn't say what happens at zero balance, or whether non-pilots get any free messaging allowance. It also doesn't say who pays for customer sign-in OTPs for non-pilot tenants: 11-integrations §3 and D7 had them 'absorbed by the platform plan', and there is no plan any more.

**Remedy**

> Decide between a prepaid wallet and charging messaging on the monthly invoice, and state the behavior at zero balance or unpaid usage (for example, falling back to email). State whether non-pilots get a messaging allowance, and say who pays for sign-in OTPs, and at what rate, now that no plan absorbs them.

        │

```

```
  send(message, tenant, cost, kind)
        │

        └─ 0 rows → notification: email only │ OTP: offer email OTP

## Failure modes

| New path | Realistic production failure | Test coverage | Error handling | User sees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CUR export → billing job | Export late or missing on the 10th | Edge case "bill not final" | Hold invoices, alert Finance, daily retry | Nothing wrong; invoice arrives later |
| Prior-month re-read | AWS credit applied in month +2 | Edge case "prior month changes" | AdjustmentLine on next invoice | A clear adjustment line |
| RLS transaction wrapper | A query runs outside the transaction | Edge case "no tenant setting" | Fail closed: zero rows | An empty result or a handled error, never another tenant's data |
| Pooled connection | Session-level SET introduced by mistake | Pooled-connection leak test + architecture ban | CI blocks the change | Nothing (caught before release) |
| Sweeper role | Role policy drifts and sees too much, or too little | Isolation test on the role; cross-tenant sweep test | Per-tenant processing still applies D4 | Holds released on time |
| Wallet debit | Burst of parallel OTP sends | Parallel-send concurrency test | Conditional UPDATE refuses; email OTP offered | "Get your code by email" |
| CloudFront routing | Functions/KVS unsupported on multi-tenant distributions | D5 spike exit criteria | Fall back to Caddy edge before build | Nothing (decided in sprint 0) |

Question: D7 — CEO-A3: How should a website refresh avoid overwriting a newer publish?

Servers also keep time in UTC, so a booking paid just after midnight in India on the 1st could slip into the previous month. We need one rule so the counts can be trusted.
Stakes if we pick wrong: a pilot misses or meets the 10-booking bar because of a date rule, and the second-city decision is made on the wrong numbers.
Recommendation: A because the goal is "bookings taken per month", which is the payment month, and it is known the moment the customer pays; IST matches how owners read their month.
Note: options differ in kind, not coverage — no completeness score.
Pros / cons:
A) Payment month, India time (recommended)
  ✅ Counts a booking the moment the customer pays, so the current month is always up to date
  ✅ Uses India time, so a booking at 00:30 on the 1st lands in the month the owner expects
  ❌ A desk booked in March for April use counts in March, not in the month it is used
B) Usage month, India time
  ✅ Matches when the space is actually used, which is how occupancy is usually read
  ✅ Prepaid bookings for later months count where the customer turns up
  ❌ Advance bookings don't show until their month arrives, so the current month looks low
Net: count when money comes in vs count when the desk is used.

Tests: captures at 23:59 and 00:01 IST on a month boundary; a next-month refund removes the earlier count. Effort S (human: ~1h / CC: ~10min). Low risk.

## Problem Statement

Independent coworking spaces in India (1–3 locations) take bookings over WhatsApp, phone and spreadsheets, and pay aggregators a large commission for customers they could serve directly. Many also lack a credible website. Cowos gives each space its own branded website and booking app. Customers pay the space directly through the space's own Razorpay account, and Cowos takes 0% commission on bookings. The full product definition is the requirements pack in `docs/requirements/` (start with `00-start-here.md`).

## Demand Evidence

- 1–3 casual conversations with coworking owners, plus SSPL's own thesis drawn from Directoo's restaurant model.
- No paid commitments, pilot sign-ups, or workflow data from any named space yet.
- Status: **unverified**. The product owner has decided to build regardless (see Premise 3).

## Status Quo

Not observed directly. The owner's view ("Actually I dont know but all the 3 options could be the case"):
- Bookings over WhatsApp, calls and Excel, with no online payment.
- Aggregator listings that take commission and own the customer.
- A basic website, with enquiries still handled by hand.
- Existing coworking software is less common.

Landscape (web search, generic terms):
- A 2023 founder interview reports myHQ takes 25–30% per booking.
- India-first operations tools already exist (Meshfloor, Engage Apps, CoWorkingOps, Omnify), so "cheap + GST" is not a unique position.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Beachhead:** independent 1–3 location coworking spaces in one city, with a meaningful share of bookings from aggregators. Hyderabad was suggested. The city is not yet confirmed.
- **Wedge:** the full website + booking app (owner's choice). No smaller paid wedge is planned.
- **Pilot:** the first 20 owners in the beachhead city get one year fully free.

## Constraints

- Fixed stack: ASP.NET Core (.NET LTS) API, React + TypeScript, PostgreSQL, AWS ap-south-1.
- "0% commission" means Cowos takes no share of a tenant's booking revenue. Customer pricing, promo codes and fees are the tenant's decision.
- Tenants create their own promo codes in the Tenant Admin, on every plan.
- Pilot terms: 12 months fully free for the first 20 owners, messaging included, no commitments required.
- Team and timeline as in the pack: about 7–8 people, MVP in about 5–6 months.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Full MVP as specified (chosen)
Build all of Phase 1 in the order of `docs/requirements/12-release-plan.md`: pilots from about week 18, launch at week 24. Effort XL, risk medium-high.

### Approach B: Booking-first, then website builder
Not chosen. Pilots without a website would wait for the second release, delaying the full branded-site pitch.

### Approach C: Pilot-first sequencing (done-for-you onboarding)
Not chosen. The reviewer recommended it, because billing and self-serve sign-up go unused during the free year. The owner preferred to build the full MVP in the specified order.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A.** It delivers the complete suite in the order the requirements pack already specifies, which is what the owner asked for. It also needs no re-sequencing of the existing release plan.

Consequences to plan for:
- Platform billing, dunning and self-serve sign-up are built before anyone pays (first payment is in month 13 for pilots). Their real-world testing starts with non-pilot tenants.
- The first pilot goes live around week 18 (month 4–5).
- The pilot year carries hosting and messaging costs for SSPL. Estimated hosting is about ₹3,600–8,000/month for 20 tenants [estimated, from the pack's ₹180–400 per tenant]. Messaging cost is unknown until pilot volumes exist.
